# Are There Any Pokemon-Only Stores in the United States?

If you have searched for a "Pokemon only store" or "Pokemon exclusive store" in the United States, you have probably found two kinds of results: official Pokemon Center pop-ups (run by The Pokemon Company in cities like New York and Seattle), and a long list of generic Trading Card Game shops that happen to carry Pokemon next to Magic, Yu-Gi-Oh, and sports cards. Almost none of those stores are actually Pokemon-only.

A true Pokemon-exclusive store is rare in America. Trainer Center HB in Huntington Beach, California is one of them, and we believe it is the first and most authentic Pokemon-only retail destination in the country.

## What "Pokemon-only" actually means

When we say Pokemon-exclusive, we mean it literally. Walk into Trainer Center HB and you will not find a Magic: The Gathering shelf. You will not find Yu-Gi-Oh booster boxes. You will not find Topps baseball cards or comic books or Funko Pops of unrelated franchises. Every product on every shelf is Pokemon. Every event on the calendar is Pokemon. Every staff conversation is about Pokemon.

That is unusual in the U.S. The standard hobby-shop model is to carry the major TCGs together because each one alone is hard to support. Pokemon, Magic, Yu-Gi-Oh, and sports cards each pull a different audience, and most shops need all four to make rent. We took a different bet: go all-in on Pokemon, become the destination for serious Pokemon collectors, and let the depth speak for itself.

## Why the rest of America has so few Pokemon-only stores

Three reasons.

**Margins are tight.** Pokemon products from distributors carry slim margins. Most shops survive by mixing in higher-margin product like singles, supplies, and other TCGs. A Pokemon-only shop has to be more disciplined about inventory and operate at higher volume to make the model work.

**Pokemon is huge but cyclical.** Pokemon goes through massive demand spikes (the Charizard era, the YouTuber boom, Scarlet & Violet, 2020 lockdowns) and slower seasons in between. Shops that diversify weather the slow seasons more easily. Pokemon-only stores have to be ready for both extremes.

**The community has been online for two decades.** eBay, TCGplayer, COMC, and Whatnot built a strong online infrastructure for Pokemon. Most modern collectors do their buying and selling online, which makes it harder for any brick-and-mortar Pokemon shop to thrive. The few that succeed lean into community, events, and trust — things online cannot replicate.

Trainer Center HB exists because that last piece matters. People want a real place to bring their kids, meet other collectors, get a card graded, talk strategy, and trade in person without parking-lot meetups or sketchy DMs.
