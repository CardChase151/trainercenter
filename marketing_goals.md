## Strategy / Playbook

### Cadence

One trade show push per month — end of May, end of June, possibly July. Each event drives the targets above; the playbook below repeats each cycle.

### Vendor outreach

**Warm list — the 31 existing vendors**

- Email sequence going out (the vendor email pipeline already exists).
- Lowest-effort, highest-conversion segment. These people have already said yes once.

**Cold list — comb the ~7K contacts for vendor profiles**

- Sift through the ~7K contacts/followers and identify ~500-1,000 who look like they could vend (active sellers, trade-night regulars, vendors at adjacent shows, etc.).
- DM each individually with an invite to apply / partner.
- Application form lets them pick **May**, **June**, or **both** events.

### At the event — vendor check-in

When each vendor arrives, Chase hands them a "Vendor Asks" card. Four asks:

1. **Send 5-10 DMs.** Encourage their network to check out Trainer Center and follow the account.
2. **Tag us tonight.** No money. Just a pic or short video of a great trade / buy / sell moment. No cash transactions captured — pure vibe content.
3. **Optional Google review.** Chance (not a requirement) to leave a review about the event as a vendor.
4. **The vision pitch.** Chase walks each vendor through the bigger plan for TC so they buy into the long-term story, not just one event night.

### At the event — guest experience

- **Google review QR cards** handed out at checkout and on the counter (using the 4×6 branded card we just regenerated, points to `g.page/r/Ca6LST9JYXmREBM/review`).
- **"Become a member" CTA** on the site → email capture → drip campaign for follow-up nurture between events.

### Posting cadence + pinning strategy

- TC is intentionally **not an "IG noise" account**. No 2-3 posts a day. The brand stays uncluttered on purpose.
- **3-5 reels per week** of varied content — event hype, vendor spotlights, trade highlights, vision-cast clips.
- **One pinned post at a time** — the current event (date + key info). Rotates when the next event takes over.
- **Light paid boost** on the pinned reel to extend organic reach.
- Last cycle, a pinned reel hit ~49k views with **none** of: paid boost, DM campaign, vendor amplification, vision-cast layer. Adding all four this cycle → 100k is plausible.

**Pin 3, push the winner.** IG allows up to 3 pinned posts at a time — use all three.

1. **Day 1 of the cycle:** post 3 candidate reels, all aimed at the same event/goal but with different hooks (e.g. "new vendor invite", "master vendor flex", "slab collectors bring the stack"). Pin all 3.
2. **Through week 1:** let them run organically. Watch views, saves, shares, comments.
3. **End of week 1:** identify the breakout — whichever is gaining the fastest.
4. **Week 2 onward:** put **all** the paid boost behind the winner. The other 2 stay pinned (still in prime real estate, still earning free views) but no ad spend behind them.

Why this beats "pin one and hope": you're letting the algorithm + audience tell you which hook actually resonates before you spend a dollar. Free A/B test, no downside.

### Hook variations / niche angles for reels

Each reel targets a different sub-segment of the audience instead of all sounding the same. Lower fatigue, broader reach, gives the algorithm more diversity to chew on. Run one angle per reel across the 3-5/week cadence.

- **"Are you new to vending?"** — first-time vendor invite, removes friction.
- **"Are you a master vendor?"** — experienced-vendor flex, status appeal.
- **"Experienced vendors — come support the new vendors and help them build inventory."** — community-first angle, gives veterans a purpose.
- **"Slab collectors with lots of graded? Bring the stack in May."** — niche to PSA/BGS collectors.
- **"Got sealed product you're trying to move? Build it up for the May event."** — sealed-product sellers.
- **"Low on cash for your next table? We have a few free spots left to get you a win."** — financial-barrier remover for vendors who can't afford a table.

### Scripted answer: "Why are you offering free tables?"

Reusable across reels, DMs, comments, and in-person at check-in. Stock answer Chase has written:

> **"Why are you offering free tables?"**
>
> Simple —
>
> We are allowing vendors to get off the ground.
>
> We want a place for people to get a few easy wins.
>
> We won't always be free, but we will have seasons or shows to build new vendors in the game we love!

This frames "free" as **mission**, not desperation. Builds trust, signals confidence, makes future paid events feel earned rather than a bait-and-switch.
