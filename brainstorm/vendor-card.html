What this is doing under the hood

Researched the mature tilt-card libraries (Atropos, vanilla-tilt.js, react-parallax-tilt). They all use the same pattern, and it's not what I was doing. Key insight:

The highlight overlays don't move. They cross-fade. Two fixed-angle linear gradients sit at opposite corners. As the card rotates in 3D, the opacity of each highlight shifts based on which side faces the (imagined) light source. The visible motion comes from the 3D rotation moving the card — the gradients themselves stay put.

That's why my earlier attempt looked like a sliding rectangle: I was sliding a colored band around, which is literally what makes things look like sliding bands. Two static highlights with opacity-driven cross-fade reads as real foil because that's how real foil actually works under a fixed light.

  • Highlight TL — linear gradient at 135° from top-left, screen blend. Opacity peaks when card tilts up-left.
  • Highlight BR — linear gradient at 315° from bottom-right, screen blend. Opacity peaks when card tilts down-right.
  • Motion — 18-second loop, ±9° tilt range (was ±24°, too aggressive), small ±2° rotateZ swirl. Subtle "breathing held card" feel, not "spinning showpiece."
  • Outer aura — kept the soft glow behind the card.
  • Removed — SVG noise grain (was causing visual artifacts), counter-tint, specular radial, shadow wash. All of that was overengineered. Less is more.

Why this loops cleanly when exported

  • Every animation is a yoyo'd sine wave on a 6–10s period. Start and end positions are identical, so a video crop on any multiple of the period seams perfectly.
  • No "sweep across and reset" animations that produce glitch frames. The parallax is always anchored to the tilt — there's no discontinuous reset.
